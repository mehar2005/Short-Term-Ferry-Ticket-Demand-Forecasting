# Short-Term Ferry Ticket Demand Forecasting and Predictive Decision Support System

## Abstract
This study develops and evaluates a short-term forecasting workflow for ferry ticket demand using historical ticket sales and redemption observations. The final abstract must be completed after running the pipeline on the official dataset and inserting measured test results.

## Keywords
Time-series forecasting; ferry demand; feature engineering; regression; predictive decision support; Streamlit.

## 1. Introduction
Short-term demand forecasts can support operational awareness by helping staff anticipate changes in ticket activity. This project investigates whether historical ticket counts and time-derived features can predict ticket demand over horizons from 15 minutes to two hours.

## 2. Problem Statement
Operational teams may benefit from timely estimates of upcoming ticket activity. The task is to use historical timestamped ticket records to forecast future ticket counts while avoiding information leakage and evaluating models on later observations.

## 3. Objectives
1. Validate the timestamped ticket dataset.
2. Explore trends and recurring time patterns.
3. Create lag, rolling-statistic, and calendar predictors.
4. Compare a persistence baseline with regression and tree-ensemble models.
5. Evaluate errors at multiple forecast horizons using chronological partitions.
6. Present model outputs and limitations through a dashboard and report.
